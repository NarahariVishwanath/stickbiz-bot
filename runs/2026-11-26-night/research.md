By Amiti Sen Updated - November 25, 2026 at 11:42 PM. | New Delhi × Share WhatsApp X Facebook LinkedIn Messenger Reddit Mail https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/economy/tariff-turmoil-us-links-lower-17-tariff-for-india-to-reciprocal-trade-deal-proposes-up-to-25-otherwise/article71743699.ece Copy Jamieson Greer, US Trade Representative The United States on Wednesday proposed a slew of fresh tariffs on 17 countries including India that could reset the negotiating table for the India-US trade deal. The US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer proposed that tariffs on Indian goods under Section 301 investigation on excess capacity could be 17 per cent if the two countries conclude a reciprocal trade pact, but rise up to 25 per cent in the absence of an agreement. A 17 per cent tariff would place India marginally below competitors such as Bangladesh, Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand and Cambodia, which face a proposed rate of 18 per cent. It would also be lower than China’s 20 per cent (in addition to existing 20-25 per cent) and Vietnam’s proposed rate of 20 per cent if Hanoi reaches a reciprocal trade agreement with Washington. However, the rate on Indian goods would still be higher than the 15 per cent proposed for Taiwan, Japan, South Korea and the European Union, leaving Indian exporters at a relative disadvantage compared with these economies. These economies also enjoy an additional advantage as their proposed rates are net of both existing Section 301 duties and Most Favoured Nation (MFN) tariffs, whereas India’s proposed rate is adjusted only for existing Section 301 tariffs. India, however, is in no hurry to seal an interim agreement with the US, as it wants to first establish the final tariff rates that Washington will impose on all competing economies and secure assurances that any tariff advantage offered to Indian exporters will be maintained in the future. Additionally, there is still the threat of sanctions that India can face under the Lindsey Graham statute on buyers of Russian oil. The proposed rates are part of the findings of the USTR’s investigation, published on Wednesday, into what it describes as structural excess capacity and production in manufacturing sectors. The USTR has determined that the industrial policies and practices of 14 economies, including India, are unreasonable and burden or restrict American commerce, making them actionable under Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974. “New Delhi wants to assess the implications of the Section 301 findings and ensure that the commitments it makes under the trade pact are matched by a durable tariff advantage over competitors,” the source said. The USTR has invited public comments on the proposed action until January 5, 2027, with hearings scheduled to begin on January 25. The final decision on imposing the duties will follow the consultation process. The proposed 17 per cent rate is an adjusted tariff proposal conditional on India reaching a reciprocal trade agreement that addresses the findings of the investigation. Under the revised framework, investors will no longer be required to furnish common supplementary KYC details separately to each intermediary, as the information will be maintained centrally in the KRA system. The move is aimed at reducing paperwork, streamlining account opening and improving the portability of KYC records across market intermediaries. “It will avoid duplication in the submission of KYC records,” said Akshaya Bhansali, Managing Partner at Mindspright Legal. “The process will become more centralised as all information will be available with KRAs. The rules are now more clearly defined and, with the circular in place, brokers and mutual funds will have greater responsibility to update KRA records.” Under the revised norms, key client information including income range, occupation, politically exposed person (PEP) status, FATCA and CRS declarations, CKYC ID, DIGIPIN and document validity details will be centrally maintained by KRAs. This is expected to eliminate the need for investors to repeatedly furnish the same information while opening accounts with multiple intermediaries. SEBI has also allowed intermediaries to update client records using information available with KRAs and the Central KYC Records Registry (CKYCRR), subject to client confirmation. The regulator said the change is intended to reduce duplication of efforts and improve consistency in investor data across institutions. In a significant relaxation, intermediaries can skip fresh mobile number verification if the KRA database already reflects the number as Aadhaar-seeded and verified. The regulator has also eased compliance requirements for resident Overseas Citizen of India (OCI) cardholders and foreign nationals by making overseas address proof optional, provided they furnish a verified Indian address. Further, several of the onboarding simplification measures will also apply to individual Persons Resident Outside India (PROI) from FATF-compliant countries. The process for name changes has also been simplified. Investors who have already updated their names in PAN and Aadhaar records will generally not be required to submit additional proof of name change to intermediaries. A key feature of the reforms is enhanced KYC portability. Records with verified details, including either the current or permanent address, and validated PAN-Aadhaar linkage will be tagged as “validated records”, enabling seamless sharing across intermediaries and significantly reducing onboarding timelines. KRAs will also be required to provide attribute-wise validation status to intermediaries, improving transparency in the KYC process. The revised framework will come into force on December 24, 2026..
