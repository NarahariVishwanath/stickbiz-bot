Mumbai: The complex deal between Tata Steel Ltd and APL Apollo Tubes Ltd could violate the Securities and Exchange Board of India's (Sebi) related-party transaction rules, three proxy advisory firms have warned, urging the market regulator to intervene. In a two-part transaction, Tata Steel first announced the sale of two of its units to APL Apollo on Sunday evening for ₹ 28,000 crore, financed through debt. On Monday morning, the two companies announced the second leg, wherein Tata Steel would acquire a controlling stake in the tubes manufacturer by buying out its promoters and acquiring more shares in a subsequent open offer. Separately, Tata Steel and the promoters of APL Apollo also agreed to enter an exclusive contract-manufacturing arrangement following their exit from the company. By structuring the transaction in this manner, the two companies ensured that Tata Steel was not a related party of APL Apollo on Sunday evening when the tubes maker decided to buy its assets, as per proxy advisors Institutional Investor Advisory Services (IiAS), Stakeholders Empowerment Services (SES), and InGovern. Thus, they avoided the requirement of seeking shareholder approval for the transaction, which is mandatory for related-party deals, the proxy advisors said. However, such deal-engineering runs afoul of Sebi’s “purpose and effect” test, which has been applicable since April 2023, they said. According to this doctrine, a deal between a listed company and a non-related party, as in this case, could be deemed as a related-party transaction, if the purpose and effect of it is to benefit a related party of the listed company. Here, even though Tata Steel was not a related party of APL Apollo at the time the latter agreed to buy the two companies from it, the deal benefitted the promoters of APL Apollo, who were a related party, the proxy advisors argue. “The transaction must be assessed as a whole, not as three legally separate announcements. The asset sale, promoter-stake sale, open offer and SG Spectra arrangement are commercially interlinked and may collectively benefit APL Apollo’s promoter group,” InGovern argued in a report dated 25 November. IiAS called upon Sebi to determine whether the purpose-and-effect test applies in this case, and to decide whether the arrangement between Tata Steel and the Gupta family , the promoters of APL Apollo, amounted to a differential payment to two sets of investors. “APL Apollo will have to borrow heavily to buy assets from the company that is about to become its promoter, and its public shareholders will carry that debt. That outcome is hard to reconcile with what the related party rules are for, even if the timing technically keeps the deal outside their scope,” IiAS noted in a report. Approval of the minority shareholders of APL Apollo must be sought before the acquisition of the two assets of Tata Steel by APL Apollo for ₹ 28,000 crore, which is several times more than what the total assets of the buyer are worth, SES noted. APL Apollo and Sebi did not immediately respond to Mint’s request for comment. The court will hear the matter on Friday (Photo: Shutterstock) The Sir Dorabji Tata Trust (SDTT) and the RD Tata Trust (RDTT) on Thursday filed suits in the Bombay High Court against Tata Sons and six of its directors, challenging the reappointment of N Chandrasekaran as executive chairman for a third five-year term. The court will hear the matter on Friday. The SDTT has sought ₹50 crore, while the RDTT has sought ₹15 crore in damages, with 18 per cent annual interest. As per the suits, the September 17 Tata Sons board resolution to reappoint Chandrasekaran was invalid because it did not receive the affirmative vote required under Article 121 of the company's Articles of Association (AoA). Senior counsel Janak Dwarkadas, appearing for the two trusts, mentioned the suits on Thursday before a single bench of Justice Arif Doctor, seeking an urgent hearing. Justice Doctor, however, recused himself without assigning any reasons, following which the suits were mentioned before Justice Abhay Ahuja, who was sitting on the alternate bench. Dwarkadas told the court that a hearing was required before the November 30 board meeting of Tata Sons. Justice Ahuja then said the pleas shall be heard on Friday. The immediate relief sought is to prevent the chairman of Tata Sons from exercising his casting vote during the November 30 board meeting in the event of a split in opinion among nominee directors. In the suits, the trusts have not sought a stay on the board meeting but a direction that Chandrasekaran does not cast his vote. The suits said Article 121 of Tata Sons' AoA does not permit the chairman of a board meeting to exercise a casting vote in cases where the articles require the affirmative vote of a majority of the trustees. The dispute follows N Chandrasekaran's reappointment as Tata Sons executive chairman for five more years at the September 17 board meeting. The suits have said the decision was "ultra vires the Articles of Association and is invalid, illegal, null and void". The two trusts voted on Wednesday night to pass the resolutions to move HC as there was a lack of any satisfactory replies to the communications sent by them since September 17. Tata Trusts, chaired by Noel Tata, holds around 66 per cent of Tata Sons and has sought a permanent order and injunction restraining Tata Sons, its directors, officers and other agents from acting upon in any manner or giving effect to the September 17 resolution reappointing Chandrasekaran. In a statement on September 20, the Trusts had said the resolution was not validly passed and had no legal effect. At the centre of the dispute are Tata Sons' AoA and special rights given to Trusts-nominated directors. It has two nominee directors on its board -- Noel Tata and Venu Srinivasan. On September 17, Noel Tata had voted against Chandrasekaran's reappointment, while Venu Srinivasan supported it. As per Trusts, both nominated directors were required to support the decision. In its statement issued in September, the Trusts said the AoA of Tata Sons provide that no decision can be taken unless it has the affirmative support of at least a majority of the Directors nominated by the Tata Trusts. This is a separate condition under the AoA, it said, pointing out that the AoA does not leave any decision of the board to a mere head count of directors.
