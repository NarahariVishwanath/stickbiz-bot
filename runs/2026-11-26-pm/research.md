GTRI calls US tariff under Section 301 coercive bargaining tool, urges India to reject it GTRI said Trump’s proposed tariffs revive a reciprocal regime under a new name, with a dual-rate structure to pressure countries into trade deals By ANI Updated - November 26, 2026 at 10:31 AM. | New Delhi × Share WhatsApp X Facebook LinkedIn Messenger Reddit Mail https://www.thehindubusinessline.com/economy/gtri-calls-us-tariff-under-section-301-coercive-bargaining-tool-urges-india-to-reject-it/article71745146.ece Copy The Global Trade Research Initiative (GTRI) has urged India to reject a proposed trade deal with the United States under the Section 301 excess-capacity investigations, alleging that Washington is using the threat of steep tariffs to extract one-sided trade concessions from its trading partners. In its report, GTRI said the proposed tariffs by US President Donald Trump effectively revive the reciprocal tariff regime under a different name, with a dual-rate structure designed to pressure countries into signing trade agreements. "The new tariffs proposed by US President Donald Trump under Section 301 excess-capacity investigations recreate the reciprocal-tariff regime under a new name," the report said. According to GTRI, the proposed framework offers lower tariff rates to countries that enter into trade agreements while imposing higher baseline duties on those that refuse to sign a pact. It argued that such an arrangement allows Washington to secure trade concessions without addressing the underlying concerns over industrial overcapacity. The report noted that India faces proposed tariffs of up to 25 per cent, which could be reduced to 17 per cent if a trade agreement is finalised. In comparison, Bangladesh faces a proposed deal rate of 18 per cent, while Vietnam and China face rates of 20 per cent. GTRI described the proposed 17 per cent rate for India as a calculated attempt to offer the country a marginal tariff advantage over competing economies rather than an objective assessment of industrial capacity. "The offering of India a 17% deal rate (versus 25% without a pact) is a calculated bait designed to show a marginal 1%-3% tariff edge over rivals like Bangladesh, Vietnam, and China," the report stated. The investigation has flagged potential excess capacity or export surpluses in seven Indian sectors, including solar modules, petrochemicals, steel, textiles, health-related goods, construction materials and automotive products. The report pointed out that India's solar module capacity is nearly three times its domestic demand, while stressing that industrial investment and production capacity should also be assessed against domestic consumption, export markets and future demand. GTRI further argued that the proposed tariff relief does not require countries to undertake any actual changes in their industrial capacity, which it said undermines the stated rationale of the investigation. "Tariff relief without reform" is the central concern highlighted in the report, which warns that countries could secure lower duties simply by granting unilateral trade concessions even if the industrial conditions prompting the investigation remain unchanged. Warning that the threat of Graham tariffs of up to 100 per cent continues to hang over India, GTRI called on New Delhi to resist pressure to enter into what it described as an unequal trade arrangement. The report recommended that India address the US allegations sector by sector and demonstrate that its industrial investments cater to domestic buyers, overseas customers and future demand. GTRI said India should defend its industrial expansion on economic grounds rather than allow export growth alone to be treated as evidence of excess capacity. ### [Business Standard] Centre asks Netflix to block streaming of Pakistani content in India
Link: https://www.business-standard.com/india-news/centre-asks-netflix-to-block-streaming-of-pakistani-content-in-india-126112600493_1.html

Centre asks Netflix to block streaming of Pakistani content in India | India News - Business Standard
The ministry has asked Netflix to adhere to its advisory dated May 8, 2025, and ensure that streaming content originating in Pakistan is not available in India | Image: Bloomberg The Information and Broadcasting ministry has received several complaints on the possible India release of Netflix's first Pakistani show "Jo Bachay Hain Sang Samait Lo" and has asked it to ensure that content originating in Pakistan is not streamed here, official sources said on Thursday. The trailer dropped on Tuesday but Netflix is yet to comment on whether the show's December 4 release includes India or is only aimed at Pakistan and other countries. Two cine worker bodies in India have written to Prime Minister Narendra Modi asking for a ban. Sources said the ministry has asked Netflix to adhere to its advisory dated May 8, 2025, and ensure that streaming content originating in Pakistan is not available in India. The ministry has also asked Netflix to stand by the commitment it gave in June -- when it informed the government that it had made no film or series originating in Pakistan available to subscribers in India -- and maintain the position it conveyed at the time. The All Indian Cine Workers Association (AICWA) and the Federation of Western India Cine Employees (FWICE) have said the show's release in the country would be a violation of the ministry's advisory, following last year's Pahalgam terror attack, when it asked OTT platforms and streaming services to discontinue Pakistani-origin content. In its letter, also marked to Information and Broadcasting Minister Ashwini Vaishnaw and Netflix India, the AICWA termed Pakistan a "terrorist country" that will never wish well for India. "After the Pulwama attack, AICWA banned Pakistani artists from working in the Indian film industry. After the Pahalgam attack, the Government of India also imposed restrictions on Pakistani artists and content," stated the letter signed by AICWA president Suresh Shyamlal Gupta. The AICWA, which states on its website that it is affiliated with the Maharashtra government and works for the rights of the people related to the film industry, went on to say that the release of the series would go directly against the spirit of that advisory and against the sentiments of the nation. The FWICE, an umbrella body of 32 associations with five lakh members, asked the prime minister to intervene and direct the ministry and other concerned authorities to urgently examine the proposed release of the Pakistani series and take appropriate measures. The FWICE has also warned that it would "ensure the shoot of all the Netflix projects comes to a halt" if the series is released in India. "We want to express that we have an objection to the Pakistani show. For us, the country comes first, and Pakistani artists are not welcome," its president B N Tiwari told PTI. In Pakistan, Planning and Development Minister Ahsan Iqbal used the hashtag #GrowUpIndia to push back and say this amounted to weaponising art and culture. "First weaponising sports, then water, and now art and culture. The All Indian Cine Workers Association is urging New Delhi to permanently ban Pakistani content. How insecure do you have to be to see a cricket match as a political threat and a television drama as a national security concern? India, grow up," Iqbal said on X. Created, directed and produced by Momina Duraid, "Jo Bachay Hain Sang Samait Lo" adapts Pakistani novelist Farhat Ishtiaq's 2013 bestselling Urdu novel of the same name. It is titled "All the Love Our Hearts Carry" in international markets. It reunites Fawad Khan, Mahira Khan and Sanam Saeed, all familiar to Indian audiences.
